# SOC-07: retail price and renewal evidence

The 13 November critical review's paired seed-73 SMB run found identical work and renewal outcomes at A$1,800 and A$1,700 per period, with only the lower cash balance changing. The optional advanced retail field therefore lacked a compensating customer benefit. This contract adds one explicit synthetic retention tradeoff for fresh Provider careers. It does not claim real market conversion rates.

At the base-stage quote, the engine shows the offer ceiling, a price exactly 5% below it, the selected price and recurring revenue given up. A price at or below that threshold changes the customer's renewal evidence requirement from two qualified full periods to one of the last three full periods. The accepted retail agreement, rather than a transient UI value, determines the rule at each renewal. The same rule applies after a manual price change at renewal. The offer still has its authored ceiling and expiry. The discount does not alter customer demand, case outcomes, credits, supplier obligations, payroll, the three-consecutive-failure exit rule, or solvency.

The `retailRetentionVersion: 1` marker opts new `provider-campaigns-8` careers into this rule. Older saves without it retain the two-period rule. The quote is pure, the purchase and renewal use validated Provider commands, and imported version values are checked. The retained agreement and agreement history carry the price and ceiling; no separate mutable retention flag is trusted.

The paired behaviour test dispatches the same paid Swift service, first repair, later incident and second repair at A$1,800 and A$1,710. Both paths deliver identical work. Of the final three periods, the first two are unqualified and the last qualifies. The full-price customer exits; the discounted customer receives a renewal offer and earns less cash. Save/reload and the visible advanced quote are checked. Browser and uncoached comprehension evidence remain pending; this is a bounded first retention path, not a complete portfolio pricing economy.
