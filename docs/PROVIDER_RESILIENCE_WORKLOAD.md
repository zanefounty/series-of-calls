# Paid inter-site work and continuity

This gameplay slice implements the source goals `progress.goal.ln` (second site connected) and `progress.goal.rd` (independent path continuity) through synthetic Provider purchases and actual customer interactions. It does not certify the broader source catalogue or approve research exclusions.

`provider-campaigns-5` retains versions 1–4 on restore. Existing foreign-guest, alias and international-call demand remains enabled in version 5. The twenty-four distinct case obligations, twenty-two on-time threshold, two consecutive qualifying periods, positive operating result and funded expansion remain unchanged.

After a paid primary inter-site service is effective, the first eligible voice case at source index zero in a subsequent complete demand period requires three sequential conversations. The first retains the mission's requested duration; two follow-ups each request three minutes. They share the real caller, tenant and case. All consumed voice usage is charged once, while the case counts once in the twenty-four-case denominator. Its response promise is explicitly extended to twenty-four minutes. The other twenty-three promises and the immutable demand plan remain unchanged. If an eligible alternate channel owns index zero, the sequence waits for a future voice case instead of overwriting that preference.

The primary domain fails one minute after the first conversation finishes. The follow-up must actually use the purchased independent backup. An acknowledged incident permits a paid engineering repair. The final conversation starts after primary recovery. Ten consecutive minute samples and actual admission/per-minute path observations establish the bounded continuity proof; simply buying a backup cannot earn it. Provider uses a scoped assessor for these three required conversations. The Training observer is unchanged, and full-period service and economic results are checked separately.

Each primary contract creates at most one sequence. Failure remains in history after a repair; repair can restore future service without rewriting the failed case. The one sequence job and its at most three actual interaction traces remain retained per customer, including case and workforce-assignment lineage. Ordinary histories still compact. A copied Lab has separate authority and finances; it cannot award a Provider claim.

All values below are authored fictional balance choices, not vendor prices. The parent treasury pays setup and recurring commitments. Child transport invoices remain empty to avoid double charging. Physical Mbps, carrier sessions, customer agents and Provider engineers remain distinct units.

Quotes include implementation effort and subsequent transport commissioning; ongoing calls can fail while a newly purchased path is not yet effective.

Development verification exercised the Steady strategy with the default Provider team: twenty-four of twenty-four cases on time, three actual conversations consuming nine billable voice minutes, a ten-minute continuity window and a positive 22,700-cent period operating result including the paid repair. Swift with the same additions and repair produced a negative incident-period result, preserving a consequential capital-versus-recurring tradeoff. These are bounded development observations, not final frozen-candidate or human-participant evidence.
