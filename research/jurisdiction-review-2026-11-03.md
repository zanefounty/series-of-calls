# Jurisdiction source review — 2026-11-03

Status: independent research review, not an owner-accepted review and not a change to any gameplay rule.

## Result

All five jurisdiction references were retrievable from the listed official source domains on 2026-11-03. Their narrowly phrased observations are supported, with the qualifications below. This does not clear them for active use: each reference still has `acceptedReview: null` and `activeJurisdictionClaim: false`; each associated claim is `observed-pending-review`; and `spec/08-jurisdiction.md` says these sources do not activate legal eligibility or settle defence classification, emergency calling, interception, national retention, or sector rules.

The Australian page itself was last reviewed on 2024-01-18. I checked newer ASD material to determine whether the central observation remains consistent; it does. The US page describes a live 2026 transition, so its applicability needs a date-specific boundary. The Japanese sources are unusually specific about service scope and AI exceptions. The EU observation is valid only for personal-data transfers within the GDPR regime and should not be read as a residency or procurement rule.

## Review blockers before any active claim

1. A named owner/reviewer must accept each claim, record the actual page and version/date reviewed, applicability, uncertainty, and linked acceptance requirement. This report does not supply that acceptance.
2. Preserve `activeJurisdictionClaim: false` and `activeInGameplay: false` until the existing gate receives the required accepted-review data. The research check date being fresh does not itself satisfy the review gate.
3. Keep scenario rules explicitly fictional. None of these sources approves the game's country profiles or supplies universal per-country storage, retention, emergency-call, interception, defence, or sector rules.
4. For US use, revisit the FedRAMP 2026 transition on/after 2027-01-01 and whenever the official timeline/rules change. Do not treat “2026 rules” as fully mandatory on the 2026-11-03 check date.
5. For Japan ISMAP, a reviewer must check the live list entry for the exact service and any applicable procurement instruction. For AI, the service or development-platform scope must be checked separately.
6. For the EU observation, keep it limited to GDPR personal-data transfers and check a current Commission decision or applicable safeguard for the specific transfer. Do not turn a transfer condition into a general region or residency predicate.

## High-value calling gap for a later work package

The mapping contains `uc-new:domain:number-porting`, requiring reservation, allocation, assignment, supplier lead times, port orders, acceptance, cutover, failed ports and recovery. The current `soc-calling-1` schemas cover canonical directory numbers, aliases, public reach, routing and emergency tests, but do not represent the number-port lifecycle; `content/calling.json` does not cite the porting domain. A bounded next package could model fictional port orders and staged cutover with provider acceptance, active-number continuity and rollback after rejection, while explicitly keeping it separate from identity, recording and workflow migration. No real carrier order or public-number action should be possible. This is a source-mapping gap, not an approved new requirement.
