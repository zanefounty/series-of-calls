# Calling, messaging and execution

An interaction records intent, participant identities, channel, destination, case identity, simulation times, configuration evidence, transitions, handling outcome, generated records, obligations and attributed costs. Aggregate workloads drive campaign utilisation; bounded representative interactions explain why a workload succeeds or fails. A protocol-looking trace is synthetic evidence from engine transitions and must say so.

## Calling model

Resolve the calling identity and entitlement before admitting a call. Interpret dialled input within the caller's tenant/site extension scope. Reject ambiguous extensions; never select an arbitrary matching endpoint. Public-number normalisation is explicit and jurisdiction/profile dependent. Emergency routing and location are distinct policies, not ordinary cheapest-route selection. The core recognises explicit E.164-like synthetic numbers and scoped extensions. The optional service-operations pack also normalises national leading-zero numbers using the configured prefix and enforces caller-number ownership, allowed prefixes and international permission. Complete country-specific plans and emergency behaviour remain pending.

Evaluate ordered routing rules and show the matching rule. Permissions can reject a route even when a destination exists. Trunk and SBC admission consume their typed session resources independently of endpoint entitlements and bandwidth. A fallback is taken only when its configured trigger applies; an unavailable destination is not permission to bypass a prohibited route. The foundation supports first-match routes and admission failure but not the complete fallback graph.

After admission, resolve signalling prerequisites such as registration, transport, identity, certificates and interworking. Then calculate outbound and inbound media separately with codec, reachability, encryption and quality constraints. Signalling may succeed while media is one-way or absent. The inspector must preserve that distinction. Voicemail, reception, shared-line service, transfers and survivability introduce their own call legs, entitlements and support responsibility; they are full-scope obligations beyond the current basic resolver.

## Messaging and CPaaS

Channel accounts identify the fictional channel, sender, market, allowed use, template approvals and throughput. A workflow consumes a synthetic event, validates account/consent/template, invokes bounded simulated APIs, waits for a receipt and routes to self-service or human handling. HTTP-like request/response evidence is generated locally; no real webhook is contacted.

Retries follow declared retryable outcomes, a deterministic schedule, maximum attempts and an idempotency scope. The same event must not create duplicate customer cases, deliveries or usage charges. Rate limiting affects throughput without destroying the underlying business need. Delivery acceptance, transport delivery, read state and customer resolution are distinct outcomes. A failed workflow retains its attempt history for diagnosis and replay.

Conversation identity persists while an asynchronous message pauses. Such a conversation does not occupy a voice session throughout. A supported cross-channel handoff links the same case and identity; unsupported or broken mappings expose context loss and add handling time/effort. Identity mismatches cannot join another customer's case. A bot that fails the task should generate escalation or repeat demand; avoiding an agent is not success by itself.

The service-operations pack implements queued delivery, authentication failures, bounded exponential retries, HTTP-like 503/429 evidence, 202 acceptance, delayed delivery receipts, signature checks and receipt deduplication. A response window permits freeform responses only after an inbound event; outside it an approved template is required. Human handoff starts after delivery. Message usage is metered once on acceptance. Consent is currently a workflow-level policy; per-contact/channel consent, arbitrary branching and read receipts remain pending.

## Aggregate demand and market substitution

Demand consists of customer needs rather than arbitrary channel counts. Channel adoption changes the distribution of those needs. An outage moves unresolved needs to another permitted channel or creates delayed/repeat demand. Voice, synchronous video, chat and asynchronous messaging use different service times and resource dimensions. Bundling can change channel choice without changing the number of underlying needs.

Aggregate arrivals must use saved deterministic seed state and the simulation clock. Recalculation cannot sample new arrivals. Detailed traces are capped and may be sampled from aggregate outcomes with an explicit sampling policy; their absence does not mean aggregate work vanished. The business pack advances deterministic aggregate demand once per billing period, carrying unresolved demand forward. Capacity uses effective skilled staffing, entitlements, shifts, handling/wrap time and digital concurrency. Manual representative traces remain separate from aggregates; they do not purport to be a sample of every aggregate arrival. Full channel substitution and industry-specific demand distributions remain pending.

## Service operations pack contracts

`soc-operations-1` is opt-in and saved with its own identity. Managed calling needs plan entitlement; operator calling needs operator approval, contract and delegation; mobile integration also needs an eligible SIM and cannot serve a queue or service caller number; gateway calling needs supported SBC, trusted TLS, hostname and peer authentication. The synthetic codec negotiation accepts Opus/G.711 and rejects the unsupported G.729 profile. Public carrier control cannot be used while disconnected. Internal extensions bypass public carrier admission. Carrier configuration and the core phone's carrier-model selection stay synchronised through provisioning.

Contact integration is independently configured: Connect requires the SBC; Extend requires API authority and an assigned app; Unify requires API authority and an embedded token. Feature declarations differ. An unsupported feature fails provisioning and preserves the previous effective configuration. Private placement currently supports Connect only. These are fictional training contracts, not vendor certifications.

Numbers progress from reserved to assigned. Porting validates the donor after two simulation minutes and cuts over no earlier than the third; rejection preserves the existing supplier. Recovery takes two minutes and retains the original port history. Cohort migration records endpoint generations and old platform placement, rechecks delegation and target readiness at cutover, provisions scopes, then verifies before committing placement. Concurrent endpoint edits block cutover. Rollback restores only a change actually applied by that project and refuses to overwrite a later edit.

## Distinct channel accounts and need outcomes

The optional `soc-channels-1` contract separates Chatterleaf Business, Letterbox Service and Casechat Web. Chatterleaf uses a purpose grant, sender approval and a finite inbound reply window, with approved templates outside the window. Letterbox requires a verified sender and a supported template but has no universal synchronous reply window. Casechat requires an authenticated canonical person, the same tenant/case binding and an unexpired session. Provider authentication, availability and local account dependencies are independent observations. Pausing a conversation allocates no voice session or staffed agent.

Check eligibility before queueing and again before each provider acceptance attempt. Record an immutable acceptance snapshot only on the simulated 202 transition. A later actual delivery receipt refers to that accepted job; later consent revocation stops new acceptance but must not erase a message already accepted or roll back its delivery event. A read receipt requires an earlier matching delivery and does not establish task completion. The central adapter owns identity/case binding and must reject a fabricated provider receipt.

The optional `soc-outcomes-1` contract records a customer need separately from transport and handling. A need has canonical tenant/person/case, a business purpose, intent, permitted channels, captured resolution criteria and a bounded retry policy. The business purpose is descriptive; it is separate from the channel permission purpose. Explicit synthetic task-result evidence must match the live interaction and the required integration. A bot reply, successful recording or delivered message alone is insufficient. Integration readiness derives from the configured effective resource and observations, not from a caller-supplied readiness flag.

Retain the technical interaction outcome, customer-need outcome and mandatory obligations independently. Unresolved needs cannot inflate resolved-contact assessment or diagnostic revenue. Repeat contact preserves the customer and case, selects an available permitted channel and uses a fresh deterministic interaction/idempotency key. A real new contact may incur new supplier usage; replay of the same attempt may not. The parent reserves repeat IDs before processing arrivals so an immediate terminal voice result cannot race the repeat-state update. Bounded attempts, exhaustion and unavailable-channel delays remain visible after save/reload.

Module cases AC-SOC-072–084 and adversarial cases AC-SOC-099–103 establish isolated mechanics. Shared command, worker, receipt and outcome integration is separately assigned AC-SOC-093–098 in the acceptance register; consult executed evidence for current verification. These mechanics do not establish the full market substitution or campaign catalogue.

## Bidirectional carrier journeys (2026-11-03 implementation increment)

The shared `interact` command accepts voice `direction: inbound | outbound`. Incoming calls carry an external synthetic E.164 caller and a called E.164 number. They require an active assigned number, an entitled holder in the selected tenant, effective platform and carrier onboarding, and a matching ordered inbound route. Reserved numbers, another provider's numbers, unprovisioned gateways, unavailable session capacity and unauthenticated carrier peers fail independently. The same IVR/queue engine handles incoming enquiries after admission. An incoming call uses the carrier's transport binding, whereas outgoing calls use the caller's binding (or the identity service binding).

Signalling acceptance does not establish media success: incoming and outgoing media directions, quality policy, endpoint registration and required information obligations remain separate checks. Incoming network/SIP admission consumes signalling capacity but does not debit an outbound destination-spend allowance. Actual billable handling still uses the ordinary usage ledger. The interaction inspector identifies all generated protocol events as synthetic. A typed journey form submits these exact commands through the selected customer console.

These changes implement bounded calling mechanics, not every source-specific calling product. The caller identity of an incoming case is the external number; a local person cannot reuse that case ID to change its identity. Save validation rejects incoming success without number-ownership, ordered-route and permission traces. Worker, persistence, command validation and calculation-purity cases accompany the implementation.

## Independent media offers

The optional media-negotiation console configures endpoint and carrier codec offers, early/delayed offers, directional media and explicitly provisioned transcoders. The dispatcher derives the route from actual calling identities, ingress carrier and destination. Missing or ambiguous profiles fail closed. A synthetic signalling success does not establish a compatible codec or either media direction.

Transcoding reserves a typed live session, independent of agent licences, available people and transport bandwidth. Wrapping a completed contact releases audio capacity. Calls reroute on an actual transfer; changed effective codec offers cause explicit renegotiation with historical session routes and synthetic offer/codec traces. Failed negotiation terminates the affected call. Repeated preflight/inspection neither reserves capacity nor advances time. Session and trace history are bounded; worker and synchronous commands use the same mechanics.
