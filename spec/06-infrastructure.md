# Physical transport and SP expansion

The upstream physical-network simulation remains available as an optional SP expansion. Preserve its geography, network graph, capacities, construction, equipment, supplier relationships, failure domains and recovery resources. The source appendix includes cities, facilities, exchanges, cables, route pairs, routers, optical systems, ports, wholesalers and commercial parameters. Original attributes remain available for reconciliation rather than being discarded during naming changes.

An MSP can initially purchase access and carrier services. A communications contract consumes transport only through explicit workload profiles. A number, paid seat, trunk session or staffed agent is not a renamed Gbit/s unit. Voice and meeting media can generate throughput using codec/rate/concurrency assumptions; the derived transport demand must remain distinguishable from the application resource that caused it.

## Physical dependencies

Sites host equipment, users, rooms and local services. Links connect sites with capacity, latency, failure domain, construction/provisioning state, ownership and maintenance responsibility. Parallel links can increase capacity while sharing a trench or cable risk. Redundancy tests remove whole failure domains rather than only one convenient edge. Local equipment needs power, space, compatible ports, optics, firmware and recovery spares.

The inherited engine routes physical traffic and models transport money separately from the new communications kernel. Its route worker, stale-result handling and synchronous fallback are preserved. Consolidation into a shared application workload and commercial ledger is pending: the preview does not claim that an SP transport change currently changes UC call quality or vice versa.

## Ordering and recovery

Supply chains create quoted cost, lead time, availability and cancellation terms. A submitted order does not add effective capacity. Construction uses crews, rights and route constraints; a delay creates revised progress and commercial impact. Repairs consume the correct spare/resource and travel/construction time. A cable ship's location and a local technician are different recovery resources.

Incidents can affect transport, call control, media, application connectors, identity, workforce or information processing. Shared infrastructure faults correlate downstream failures. The inspector must expose this correlation rather than show unrelated random outages. After restoration, queues and processing backlogs resume within capacity; repair does not retroactively turn failed interactions into successes.

## Site continuity and recovery (2026-11-03 implementation increment)

The optional site-operations pack links a site's desired configuration to actual transport sites, local administration resources, endpoints, backup capacity and restore infrastructure. Ownership is derived from the commercial resource owner and active delegation; a label declaring a customer-owned site is insufficient. Regions, power domains, site-failure domains and shared link domains must match their effective transport counterparts. Site submissions provision on a subsequent simulation minute, preserving the previous effective configuration if prerequisites fail.

Continuity exercises retain a pre-fault configuration snapshot and prove a real cross-site outgoing call before injecting a canonical transport fault. While the target site is affected, an incoming call from the configured carrier ingress must reach an endpoint at the surviving site using an available path. Recovery clears that fault; a separate restore command stages the captured resource configuration, waits for provisioning, compares restored values, and requires another successful journey. The stronger diversity goal requires power and whole-site failures targeted at each site plus a shared-domain failure on the same pair. Independent backup-power domains are optional and must be observed available; bandwidth does not create sessions or staff.

Endpoint migrations retain per-resource completion receipts and update actual transport bindings. Partial completion remains visible and retries skip completed items. The console provides typed site, binding, exercise, recovery, restore and migration forms. The parent command dispatcher remains the validation boundary for UI, automation and worker execution. This pack does not claim complete physical SP construction or every mapped asset's distinctive behaviour.
