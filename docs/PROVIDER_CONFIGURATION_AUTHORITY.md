# Provider configuration authority

`SOC_PROVIDER_CONFIGURATION_AUTHORITY` replays customer resource configuration from the pinned campaign plan, the canonical resource catalogue, and the customer's submitted paid stages and operations. Provider save validation and worker validation call the same pure helper. Desired configuration must equal the replayed result; effective configuration may be the immediately preceding authorized version while a real provisioning change is pending.

The replay checks the complete resource inventory and typed, nested desired/effective configuration, including identity entitlements, endpoint identity and scope, dependencies, workforce flags, and product deployment bindings. For resource writes represented in the replay, the saved provisioning due cannot precede the latest authored paid write, and ready state cannot precede its due. Some special resource writers (including retained-storage and incident paths) have their own parent-level receipts; this helper does not claim exact timing coverage for every such writer. Numeric capacity fields remain governed by `SOC_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_AUTHORITY`.

Product offers and deployments must match a canonical recipe and a paid product project for the same customer, linked to its submitted Provider product task and operation receipt. A first deployment’s due is checked against the submitted paid project, a reconfiguration preserves the prior effective generation while its next due follows the recorded effective-history write, and a paid reinstatement must match the retirement and recommission receipts. Ready/effective deployment state is refused before the causal due. A retry due is accepted only when it follows the Provider’s saved five-minute retry cadence and the same submitted task. The product pack continues to validate deployment structure and retirement lineage. Product retries do not have a dedicated immutable command-history receipt, so this helper validates their saved schedule and task lineage, not a cryptographic record of the retry command.

The helper accepts `{ catalogue }` as its fourth argument so the catalogue remains owned by the communications kernel. It does not trust saved command history or a saved configuration hash. Pending enterprise migration scope changes are accepted only when the provider task is linked to its paid migration or dedicated-product project and an actual migration record; both sides of the in-flight endpoint scope are retained until completion.

Incident fault and connectivity replay remains in the Provider incident authority hook. This helper does not establish cryptographic tamper proofing for exports, source-catalogue completeness, external certification, or historical legal approval.
